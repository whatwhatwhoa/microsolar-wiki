# IEC TS 62257, the Multi-Tier Framework and VeraSol: research notes

Research for the picogrid code (off-grid DC solar, up to about 5 kWh storage, 12/24 V now, 48 V
later, homes and small community buildings). Researched 2026-11-23.

How to read this file:

- Every claim carries a source tag such as [W-6] or [P-200]; the Sources list at the end gives the
  URL and the source class.
- Source classes: **primary** (the standard or its official free preview, read as text),
  **official abstract** (IEC webstore product page), **secondary** (summaries by others).
- IEC texts are paywalled. Free previews stop after the scope, the definitions and sometimes the
  first clause. Where a value or clause is not in a preview or abstract, this file says
  **not found**. No clause number or value here comes from memory.
- "My estimate" marks arithmetic or judgement that is mine, not a source's.

## Key findings for the code

1. **The series is being renumbered.** IEC TS 62257-100:2022 moves the whole series to three-digit
   part numbers in five groups: -1xx introduction and project management, -2xx system design and
   installation, -3xx energy system components, -4xx user equipment and appliances, -5xx
   stand-alone products and packaged kits [P-100]. Old parts get new numbers at their next
   edition [P-100]. Since 2022 four old parts have been replaced (-1 by -100, -4 by -200, -7-4 by
   -301, -8-1 by -341), one new part has appeared (-350, inverters) and -7-1 (PV generators) has
   been withdrawn with no replacement named [W-1, W-4, W-7-4, W-8-1, W-350, W-7-1]. The new
   series title is "Renewable energy off-grid systems" [P-100].
2. **The five groups are a ready-made skeleton for the code**: plan, design and install, components,
   loads and appliances, packaged kits. The lifecycle parts (-3 project and documentation, -6
   acceptance, operation, maintenance and replacement) cut across them.
3. **The user-installation parts stop at 500 VA.** -9-3 (user interface) and -9-4 (user
   installation) apply only up to 500 VA; above that, -9-3 says -5 applies [W-9-3, P-9-3]. A 1-5 kWh
   picogrid with a 1-3 kVA inverter is above that line, so the code must fill this gap from -5,
   -9-1 and IEC 60364 rather than from -9-4.
4. **-9-1 (micropower systems) covers ELV DC plants below 120 V and individual systems**, not only
   village AC plants [P-9-1]. Its outline (battery room or enclosure, marking for emergency
   services and maintenance, four-phase acceptance with commissioning records) fits a picogrid well.
5. **-200:2026 gives the design method in four steps**: resource assessment, load assessment, choice
   of plant type and components, and a monitoring plan [P-200]. It keeps the architecture types
   from -2 (T1I to T6I for individual systems; the collective types are renamed T1M to T6M) [P-200,
   P-2]. A home picogrid is type T2I, "individual electrification system, REN generation with
   energy storage" [P-200].
6. **VeraSol / IEC TS 62257-9-8 stops at 350 W, 35 V and DC only** [W-9-8, P-9-8]. No quality
   standard was found for larger solar home systems or for systems with inverters.
7. **Tier names clash.** The wiki's "Tier 0-3" does not match the World Bank MTF tiers 0-5 that NGO
   readers know, and -200:2026 adds a third scheme with headings "Tier 1" to "Tier 7" in its
   Annex E [P-200, MTF]. Recommendation: drop the word "Tier" for system sizes (see the MTF section).

### 1.3 Corrections to the prior study

- **-8-1:2018 is no longer in force.** It is replaced by IEC TS 62257-341:2026, same subject
  (automotive flooded lead-acid batteries) [W-8-1, W-341]. -341 raises the applicable voltage levels
  and removes the 100 kW power limit [W-341].
- **-9-8:2020 is replaced by -9-8:2025 (edition 2.0).** Changes listed in the redline preview:
  recurring testing every three years instead of two; truth-in-advertising tolerance tightened from
  15 % to 10 % for numbers other than luminous flux; simpler rules for 12 V and 5 V ports; new rules
  for USB fast-charging ports; clarified single-cell lithium battery safety [P-9-8]. VeraSol moved to
  the new editions of -9-5 and -9-8 on 2025-07-01 [VS-1].
- **-9-5 edition 5.0 (2024) is confirmed** [W-9-5].
- **-5:2015 and -9-4:2016 are still in force**, both with stability date 2028 [W-5, W-9-4].
- **The "12/24 V" figure for -9-4 is not in its abstract.** The abstract says single-phase
  installations up to 500 VA, supplied by an AC microgrid or by the user's own single-unit AC or DC
  micropower plant [W-9-4]. Not checked further.
- **-7-1 (PV generators) was withdrawn on 2026-07-13** with no replacement named [W-7-1]. -350:2025
  still lists -7-1:2010 as a normative reference, next to IEC 62548 (PV arrays, design
  requirements) [P-350]. -9-1 also cites IEC 62548 [P-9-1]. My suggestion: cite IEC 62548 for PV
  array design and keep -7-1 only as background.

### 1.4 Conflicts between sources

- **Voltage scope.** -100, -200 and the 2015/2016 parts cover systems up to 1 000 V AC and 1 500 V
  DC [P-100, P-200, P-2]. The -9-5:2024 abstract and the -9-8:2025 introduction still say "AC
  nominal voltage below 500 V, DC nominal voltage below 750 V and nominal power below 100 kVA"
  [W-9-5, P-9-8], which is the older series scope that the 2015 editions removed [W-2].
- **Architecture labels.** -2:2015 names the collective types T1C to T6C [P-2]; -200:2026 names them
  T1M to T6M ("microgrid") [P-200]. Both parts are in force. The individual types (T1I to T6I) keep
  their names.
- **Tier labels.** -200:2026 Annex E has headings "Tier 1 - Portable lighting" through "Tier 7 -
  Large communal or industrial" under the title "Classification of systems with reference to the
  ESMAP World Bank tier structure" [P-200]. The ESMAP Multi-Tier Framework itself has Tiers 0 to 5
  [MTF]. The annex body is not in the preview, so how IEC maps its seven headings to the six MTF
  tiers is **not found**.
- **VeraSol appliance certification.** VeraSol's 2025 appliance process describes testing and
  third-party review of results, not certification [VS-4]; a 2026 CLASP funding guidance document
  (seen only as a search snippet, not read) says certification is required for solar water pumps
  and refrigerators. Treat as unresolved.

## 2. Parts that shape the code's structure

### 2.1 IEC TS 62257-100:2022, Overview (replaces -1:2015)

Read: the VDE preview (7 pages) and the iTeh sample (11 pages) [P-100].

- **Scope.** Introduces the whole series for off-grid renewable and hybrid products and systems
  "most commonly used for rural applications and access to electricity", in developing or developed
  countries, "the only difference being the level of service and the needed quantity of energy that
  the customer can afford" [P-100].
- **Series limits.** Off-grid renewable and hybrid systems, including micro-grids, up to 1 000 V AC
  or 1 500 V DC [P-100].
- **Aim of the series.** "The minimum sufficient recommendations, including items for safety,
  sustainability of systems and at the lowest life cycle cost" [P-100].
- **Two kinds of system.** Packaged products (from solar lanterns to self-install solar home kits,
  evaluated by parts of the series) and custom systems designed for local needs, including hybrids
  and micro-grids [P-100].
- **Intended readers** include "installers of small PV and storage" and "system owners, residents,
  operators, maintenance personnel" [P-100], which matches the wiki's readers.
- **Numbering scheme (Table 1).** -100 overview; -11x project management framework; -12x tendering
  and evaluation; -13x preliminary assessments; -20x design; -21x installation; -22x tools for
  installation; -23x competency evaluation; -30x generators and energy conversion; -31x PV modules;
  -32x wind turbines; -33x generator sets; -34x batteries and BMS; -35x inverters; -36x balance of
  system, DC; -37x balance of system, AC; -40x off-grid loads; -41x lighting; -50x stand-alone
  renewable energy products [P-100].
- **Definitions given in the preview.** Competency ("demonstration of appropriate technical knowledge
  and relevant skills to perform the designated tasks and responsibilities"); generator; hybrid
  system; regional/national/utility grid; off-grid ("electrified solely by stand-alone operation of
  the energy system ... and not in parallel with a utility grid"); renewable energy; ESMAP [P-100].
  Each new-scheme part defines its own terms; -100 drops the series-wide glossary annex of -1 [P-100].
- **Annex A** is a look-up table from old part numbers to new subtopics; its content is not in the
  preview (**not found**).
- **Old -1:2015**: the abstract says Parts 2 to 6 give methodological support for project
  management and Parts 7 to 12 give technical specifications for systems and components [W-1].

### 2.2 IEC TS 62257-2:2015, From requirements to a range of electrification systems

Read: the VDE preview (8 pages: contents, foreword, introduction, scope) [P-2]; abstract [W-2].

- **Scope.** A method for socio-economic studies in rural electrification projects, aimed at project
  teams; detail scales with project size ("for a project which involves a single or few households,
  the study could be truncated"). It also proposes system structures matched to qualitative and
  quantitative energy demand and to "the needs and financial situation of the customers" [P-2].
- **Structure (from the contents).** Clause 5 "Classification of electrification systems": user
  requirements; qualitative requirements (type of desired use, availability, quality of supply);
  quantitative requirements; "classification for electricity services provided"; assisted selection
  of the production subsystem; typology of systems. Clause 6: architecture, split into production,
  distribution and user ("demand") subsystems. Annex B analyses loads for homes, health centres,
  places of worship, community centres, schools, administrative premises, communication, public
  lighting, pumping and battery charging stations. Annex C gives supply quality indicators [P-2].
- **Architecture types (Annex E headings).** T1I: REN with no storage; T2I: individual system, REN
  with storage; T3I: REN + diesel without storage; T4I: RE + diesel with storage; T5I: genset only;
  T6I: genset with storage; T1C to T6C: the same for collective systems feeding a microgrid [P-2].
- **Numbers.** The quantitative categories (Table 3 "Synthesis of quantitative requirements/category")
  and the service classes (Table C.1, C.2) are not in the preview: **not found**.
- -200:2026 says design-relevant content of -2 now lives in -200 [P-200].

### 2.3 IEC TS 62257-3:2015, Project development and management

Read: the VDE preview (10 pages: contents, scope, normative references, first definitions) [P-3];
abstract [W-3].

- **Scope.** Responsibilities of the participants, contracts between them, tests for renewable and
  hybrid systems, quality assurance principles, recycling and environmental protection [P-3, W-3].
- **Contracts covered (contents).** Developer-implementer, implementer-subcontractor, owner-operator,
  operator-user, owner-developer, owner-user, developer-engineering consultant, developer-training
  provider; verification of commitments; consequences of non-adherence [P-3].
- **Documentation the user receives (contents).** 5.8 "Operational/technician documentation" with
  5.8.2 "System installation manual" and 5.8.3 "Users' basic operation manual" [P-3]. Annex A covers
  technical guarantees, sizing, design, procurement, installation, commissioning, operator training,
  a user training syllabus, contractual warranty, maintenance contract, replacement of components
  and maintenance organisation [P-3]. The content of these clauses is **not found**.
- **Tests (contents).** Safety tests (including verification of a residual-current device),
  climatic and mechanical tests (impact, overturning), EMC tests; normative references include
  IEC 60068 environmental tests, IEC 60529 (IP), IEC 62262 (IK), IEC 60364-6 (verification) [P-3].
- **Definition.** "General specification": prepared by the project developer, mainly defining "the
  level and cost of services to be reached and project conditions" [P-3].
- Clause 8 covers environmental protection, recycling of components and decommissioning [P-3].

### 2.4 IEC TS 62257-200:2026, System selection and design (replaces -4:2015)

Read: the iTeh sample (13 pages: contents, foreword, introduction, scope, definitions, start of
clause 4) [P-200]; abstract [W-200].

- **What changed from -4.** -4 covered only systems supplied through projects; -200 also covers
  systems "supplied directly to a user by a system supplier". Design content moved in from -2.
  Plant typologies are updated for newer inverter functions [P-200].
- **Scope and method.** A way to describe "the results to be achieved by the electrification system
  independently of the technical solutions" [P-200, W-200]. The design steps are: (1) resource
  assessment (data sources named: PVGIS, Global Solar Atlas, NASA POWER); (2) load assessment;
  (3) choose the plant type and specify components; (4) "develop a monitoring plan that will
  support the ongoing operation and maintenance" [P-200].
- **Definitions (selection).** Individual electrification system (IES): "small electric power station
  (micropower plant system) that supplies electricity to one consumption point, such as a household,
  usually from a single energy resource". Isolated microgrid: plant plus distribution "that supplies
  electricity to multiple consumption connections". User electrical installation: "protection
  devices, switchgear and wiring that provides power from a microgrid or from a micropower plant to
  the user's electrical appliances". Battery system: one or more cells, modules or batteries, which
  can include BMS and protective equipment [P-200].
- **Requirements tables.** For each participant (developer, implementer, operator, maintenance
  contractor, designer, users), Tables 1-4 list what they need. For an IES (Table 2) the list for
  the owner or user includes standardised equipment, "reliable and easy-to replace on site
  equipment", "limited spare parts variety", "simple operational rules" and demand-side management
  [P-200]. Requirements "shall be identified" for each design factor; not every characteristic in
  Tables 5 and 6 applies to an IES [P-200].
- **Contents useful for the code.** Clause 7: type of use, availability, quality of supply, power and
  energy needs, energy reduction strategies, energy management for individual systems. Clause 8:
  specification tables for the PV array and its structure, battery system, inverters, charge
  controllers, cables, switchgear and protection, energy outputs, cost presentation, "design
  warranty", steps against climatic hazards. Clause 9: data to measure and record per component.
  Annex C: user load questionnaire. Annex D: supply quality indicators (power quality,
  availability). Annex F: architectures T1I to T6I and T1M to T6M, with DC-coupled and AC-coupled
  variants of T2I [P-200].
- **Numbers.** Design margins, sizing rules, autonomy days and the content of the specification
  tables are not in the preview: **not found**.

### 2.5 IEC TS 62257-6:2015, Acceptance, operation, maintenance and replacement (AOMR)

Read: the VDE preview (7 pages: contents, scope, references, definitions, start of 4.1) [P-6];
abstract [W-6].

- **Scope.** Rules for acceptance, operation, maintenance and replacement of decentralised rural
  systems "to meet basic needs" at isolated dwellings, village houses, community services (public
  lighting, pumping, health centres, places of worship, administrative buildings) and economic
  activities. "It does not substitute for technical manuals provided by manufacturers." "The
  complexity of the system and application will dictate the level of required AOMR documentation"
  [P-6].
- **System categories.** Process electrification (e.g. pumping); individual electrification systems
  (single user, load or application); collective systems [P-6, P-9-1].
- **Structure (contents).** System identification and operational record keeping; limits of the
  AOMR domain; planning and traceability of actions; acceptance; "recommended practices for
  intervention"; maintenance actions; replacement factors; "analysis of the conformity of the
  delivered service to the contractual commitments"; the same set again per item of electric
  equipment, including an acceptance test [P-6].
- **Tables named.** Table 2 "System acceptance process description"; Table 4 "Levels of skill for
  safety"; Table 5 "Levels of general skill"; Table 6 "Maintenance actions planning - example";
  Table 7 "Voltage domains"; Table 8 "Verification of the adherence to commitments" [P-6].
- **Numbers.** Maintenance intervals, skill-level definitions and acceptance criteria: **not found**.

### 2.6 IEC TS 62257-7-1:2010, PV generators (withdrawn 2026-07-13)

Read: the iTeh sample (15 pages: contents, scope, references, definitions) [P-7-1]; webstore page
[W-7-1].

- **Scope.** Design and safety of PV generators in rural systems, for ELV and LV arrays; protection
  for "uninformed persons", "informed workers (e.g. electricians)" and "emergency workers (for
  example fire fighters)". Installation of PV arrays refers to IEC 60364-7-712 [P-7-1].
- **Voltage domains (Table 1).** ELV: Un ≤ 50 V and Uoc ≤ 120 V. LV: 50 V < Un ≤ 1 000 V and
  120 V < Uoc ≤ 1 500 V. ELV is "not exceeding 50 V a.c. and 120 V d.c. ripple free" [P-7-1].
  For the code: a 48 V picogrid whose PV string Voc exceeds 120 V leaves the ELV domain (my reading
  of Table 1).
- **Configurations (figure titles).** Earthing configurations for PV-only IES "P < 500 W" with and
  without inverter, split by cable distance d < 15 m and d > 15 m between array and technical room
  [P-7-1]. The rules behind the 15 m split are **not found**.
- **Other contents.** Overcurrent protection for strings, SPDs, disconnecting means, blocking and
  bypass diodes, cable sizing; acceptance by open-circuit voltage and short-circuit current
  measurement with commissioning records; Annex B "Example of maintenance schedule"; signs for
  junction boxes, disconnectors and fire emergency information; case studies of ELV arrays with and
  without battery [P-7-1].

### 2.7 IEC TS 62257-9-1:2016, Micropower systems

Read: the VDE preview (9 pages: contents, scope, references) [P-9-1]; abstract [W-9-1].

- **Scope.** The generating subsystem ("micropower plant") of a rural system: design, erection,
  operation and safety, ≤ 100 kVA, no voltage transformation. Voltage levels: 240/415, 230/400,
  220/380 and 120/208 V AC, **and "the ELV (less than 120 V) d.c. systems"**. It covers centralised
  plants for process, individual and collective systems, not distributed generation on microgrids
  [P-9-1]. The abstract mentions only AC [W-9-1]; the preview scope is wider.
- **Three subsystems.** Individual and process systems have a generation subsystem and the user's
  installation; collective systems add a distribution subsystem (microgrid) [P-9-1].
- **Contents useful for the code.** Design criteria and system voltage selection; technical room;
  battery room; safety (electrical, mechanical, thermal and fire, noise, access security); siting of
  PV array, technical room and battery enclosure; equipment installation [P-9-1].
- **Acceptance (clause 8).** Phase 1 preparation; phase 2 documentation; phase 3 commissioning in four
  steps (conformity of the installed system, qualification of the installation, preliminary tests,
  performance testing); phase 4 agreement; commissioning records; Annex G example record sheets
  [P-9-1].
- **Marking and documentation (clause 10).** Information for emergency services, for maintenance and
  for batteries; signs; documentation [P-9-1].
- **Annexes.** A: selectivity of protection; B: lightning risk assessment (simplified and
  multi-criteria, with stake, construction, height, situation and lightning prevalence indexes);
  C (normative): voltage domains; D: battery room and battery enclosure examples, including venting
  to outside; E: energy fraction calculations; F: noise [P-9-1].
- **Tables named.** Table 1 minimum dimensions of lightning protection wires; Table 2 cable
  cross-sections for 230 V AC; Tables 3-5 fuse and breaker ratings for AC circuits [P-9-1]. Values:
  **not found**.

### 2.8 IEC TS 62257-9-2:2016, Microgrids

Read: the VDE preview scope [P-9-2]; abstract [W-9-2].

- **Scope.** Low-voltage AC microgrids, single- or three-phase, ≤ 100 kVA, fed by one micropower
  plant, radial, mainly overhead lines ("in particular cases, underground cables can be used"), at
  the same AC voltages as -9-1 [P-9-2]. It does not cover DC distribution. For the picogrid code it
  matters only for a future shared community AC grid.

### 2.9 IEC TS 62257-9-3:2016, User interface

Read: the VDE preview scope [P-9-3]; abstract [W-9-3].

- The interface equipment (distribution board) in the user's installation, connecting it to an AC
  microgrid or "the generating part - a.c. or d.c. - of a standalone installation". It provides
  protection, isolation and distribution. Limit: installations up to 500 VA. "For installations
  above 500 VA in decentralized electrification systems, IEC TS 62257-5 applies" [P-9-3].

### 2.10 IEC TS 62257-9-6:2019, Selection of PV individual electrification systems

Read: the iTeh sample (13 pages: contents, scope, clauses 3 to 6.1) [P-9-6]; abstract [W-9-6].

- **Scope.** A simple selection procedure and cheap comparative tests, run in local laboratories, to
  pick the best PV-IES model up to 500 Wp for a project. "This document is not a type approval
  standard" [P-9-6]. The foreword says the 2019 edition removed the limits on system voltage and
  power [P-9-6]; the scope still says "up to 500 Wp" (internal inconsistency).
- **System boundary.** PV generator with support structure, charge controller, storage with casing,
  wiring with switches and protective devices (refers to -9-3), and the loads for the service [P-9-6].
- **Rules visible in the preview.** Components "shall comply with the relevant IEC standards and/or
  the relevant local regulations"; batteries selected by the -8-1 tests (now -341) and lamps by
  -12-1; safety per -5; installation per -7-1, -9-4 "and any local wiring codes"; operation and
  maintenance per -6 [P-9-6].
- **Suggested minimum enclosure rating.** IP 34 (Table 2); the IK value is unreadable in the
  preview (**not found**) [P-9-6].
- **Example climate (Table 1).** Operating -10 °C to +50 °C; storage and transport -40 °C to +80 °C;
  humidity 5 % to 95 %; pressure 860 hPa to 1 060 hPa [P-9-6].
- **User documentation.** "A manual shall also be provided" covering initial operations before first
  use, proper use, mounting the PV module, charging instructions, maintenance and troubleshooting
  [P-9-6].
- **Pre-selection.** Manufacturer's service claims; PV modules to IEC 61215 and IEC 61730-1/-2;
  batteries and lamps to relevant standards; battery casing [P-9-6].
- **Tests.** Test 1 initial inspection and commissioning; Test 2 service under favourable daylight;
  Test 3 service under unfavourable daylight; Test 4 final inspection; all samples tested at the same
  time in the same local conditions. Service quality indexes for lighting, radio/TV and refrigeration
  [P-9-6].
- **Definitions.** Duration of service ("number of hours when a load is powered"); fulfilment of
  service ("ratio of a measured provided service to a required service"); service ratio; reference
  irradiation [P-9-6].
- **Size examples (Annex C titles).** Small PV-IES ~50 Wp, medium ~200 Wp, large ~500 Wp [P-9-6].
  The load lists themselves are **not found**.

### 2.11 IEC TS 62257-12-1:2020, Lamps and lighting appliances

Read: the VDE preview (11 pages: contents, scope) [P-12-1]; abstract [W-12-1].

- **Scope.** A framework for product specifications (minimum quality standards, warranty
  requirements, performance criteria) for lamps and lighting appliances in off-grid systems. Supply:
  AC up to 250 V or DC up to 48 V; light source CFL, linear fluorescent or LED; lamps without their
  own battery or energy source [P-12-1].
- **Contents.** Quality test method (QTM) and market check method (MCM); Annex A example quality
  standards for market-support programmes and bulk procurement; Annex C (normative) manufacturer
  self-reported information [P-12-1]. Threshold values: **not found**.

### 2.12 New component parts: -301, -341, -350

- **-341:2026 (car batteries).** Comparative tests for flooded lead-acid automotive batteries,
  including with a "smart battery charging system"; plus installation and safety rules: packing and
  shipping, environment, battery housing, electrolyte hazard, prevention of short circuits, battery
  enclosures, final inspection, safety information, administrative formalities, recycling; marking
  for spillage prevention (Figure 8); references ISO 7010 safety signs [P-341, W-341].
- **-350:2025 (inverters).** Selection and sizing of off-grid inverters per architecture: VFD for
  T1I-c (no battery), unidirectional grid-forming for T2I (battery), grid-following for T3I,
  bidirectional grid-forming for T4I. General considerations: IP rating, ambient temperature,
  altitude, direct sunlight, efficiency, "no-load and stand-by power requirements", ventilation,
  earthing, waveform quality, harmonics, EMI, "load growth", protection [P-350]. References
  IEC 62109 (converter safety), IEC 61683 (efficiency), IEC 62548 [P-350]. Values: **not found**.
- **-301:2025 (hybrids).** Three hybrid mini-grid types: multi-master rotating-machine dominated,
  single switched master, multi-master inverter dominated [P-301, W-301]. Relevant only if a
  generator is added.

## 3. World Bank / ESMAP Multi-Tier Framework (MTF)

Read: "Beyond Connections: Energy Access Redefined" (ESMAP 2015), executive summary Tables ES.1 to
ES.3 and full report Tables 6.3, 6.13 and 10.4 [MTF, MTF-ES]. Primary source.

### 3.1 Household electricity supply (Table ES.1)

| Attribute | Tier 0 | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 | Tier 4 | Tier 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Power capacity (minimum) | below Tier 1 | 3 W (very low power) | 50 W (low power) | 200 W (medium) | 800 W (high) | 2 kW (very high) |
| AND daily capacity (minimum) | below 12 Wh | 12 Wh | 200 Wh | 1.0 kWh | 3.4 kWh | 8.2 kWh |
| OR services | - | lighting 1 000 lm·h/day and phone charging | electrical lighting, air circulation, TV, phone charging | - | - | - |
| Hours per day (minimum) | - | 4 h | 4 h | 8 h | 16 h | 23 h |
| Hours per evening (minimum) | - | 1 h | 2 h | 3 h | 4 h | 4 h |
| Reliability | - | - | - | - | max 14 disruptions/week | max 3 disruptions/week, total < 2 h |
| Quality | - | - | - | - | voltage problems do not affect desired appliances | same |
| Affordability | - | - | - | 365 kWh/year package costs < 5 % of household income | same | same |
| Legality | - | - | - | - | bill paid to utility, prepaid seller or authorised agent | same |
| Health and safety | - | - | - | - | no past accidents, no perceived high risk | same |

The column placement of the reliability to safety rows follows the printed layout of Table ES.1
[MTF-ES]. The report notes the watt values are "indicative, particularly for Tier 1 and Tier 2, as
the efficiency of end-user appliances is critical" [MTF-ES].

### 3.2 Services and consumption

- **Services (Table ES.2).** Tier 1: task lighting and phone charging. Tier 2: general lighting, TV,
  fan if needed. Tier 3: Tier 2 and any medium-power appliances. Tier 4: plus any high-power
  appliances. Tier 5: plus any very high-power appliances [MTF-ES].
- **Consumption (Table ES.3).** Annual minimum 4.5, 73, 365, 1 250 and 3 000 kWh for Tiers 1 to 5;
  daily 12, 200, 1 000, 3 425 and 8 219 Wh [MTF-ES].
- **Below Tier 1.** Pico-solar devices that do not reach Tier 1 earn fractional credit between Tier 0
  and Tier 1, with lighting benchmarks at 0, 100 and 1 000 lumen-hours per day [MTF-ES].

### 3.3 How technologies map to tiers

- Table 6.3 lists "typical supply technologies": Tier 1 solar lantern; Tier 2 rechargeable battery,
  SHS; Tier 3 medium SHS, fossil-fuel generator, mini-grid; Tier 4 large SHS, generator, mini-grid,
  central grid; Tier 5 large generator, central grid [MTF].
- Capacity "is measured in watts for grids, mini-grids, and fossil-fuel-based generators, and in
  watt-hours for rechargeable batteries, solar lanterns, and solar home systems" [MTF].
- The MTF measures what a household receives (seven attributes, surveyed at the household); a
  product's tier is only its capacity contribution. "The lowest tier among all attributes determines
  the overall access to electricity tier for the household" [MTF].
- **Community buildings (Table 10.4).** The same 3 W / 50 W / 200 W thresholds for Tiers 1 to 3; at
  Tier 4 "800 W for small facilities (up to three rooms) or 2 kW for large facilities (over three
  rooms)"; at Tier 5 "2 kW or 10 kW"; daily capacity as for households (12 Wh to 8.2 kWh) [MTF].

### 3.4 Where the wiki's tiers fall (my estimate)

Assumptions (mine): 4 to 5 peak sun hours, about 70 % from panel to load (battery, controller,
inverter and wiring losses), a 24 V inverter of at least 800 W for Wiki Tiers 1 and 2.

| Wiki label | Hardware | Daily energy (my estimate) | MTF capacity tier it can support |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wiki Tier 0 | 0.2-0.5 kWh storage kit | about 0.1-0.4 kWh | MTF Tier 1-2 |
| Wiki Tier 1 | one 400-500 W panel, 1-2.5 kWh, 24 V | about 1.1-1.75 kWh | MTF Tier 3 |
| Wiki Tier 2 | 2-3 panels, 2.5-5 kWh, 24 V | about 2.2-5 kWh | MTF Tier 3, Tier 4 at the top end |
| Wiki Tier 3 | 48 V, 5+ kWh, community | 5 kWh and more | MTF Tier 4-5 (household); community Table 10.4 |

The clash is direct: the wiki's "Tier 0" kit gives what MTF calls Tier 1-2, while MTF "Tier 0"
means no access. An NGO reader who sees "Tier 1 system" will read "solar lantern level", not a
500 W panel with a 2 kWh battery. -200:2026 Annex E adds a third numbering (Tier 1 to Tier 7)
[P-200].

### 3.5 Recommendation: naming and alignment

1. **Stop calling system sizes "Tier".** MTF tiers are outcomes measured at the household across
   seven attributes [MTF-ES]; the wiki's tiers are hardware sizes. The same hardware gives different
   MTF tiers depending on sun, loads, reliability and affordability. Two numbering schemes with the
   same word will be misread by the readers who matter most (NGO leads, funders who report SDG 7
   progress in MTF tiers).
2. **Use a neutral size name.** Suggested: "Size A, B, C, D" (or S, M, L, XL). Avoid "Class" (IEC
   uses Class I, II, III for shock protection of equipment [P-7-1]) and avoid "Type" (IEC uses T1I to
   T6M for architectures [P-200]).
3. **State the MTF tier each size is designed to deliver**, as a design target in the size table
   ("Size B: designed for MTF Tier 3 capacity, at least 200 W and 1.0 kWh/day, 8 h/day, 3 h in the
   evening"). This lets NGOs map the wiki to their reporting without a second vocabulary, and the
   code can then require the duration and evening-hours targets of that tier, not only kWh.
4. **State the IEC architecture type**: home picogrids are T2I (individual system, REN with storage)
   [P-200]; a community building on its own system is also an IES; a shared network is T2M.
5. **For community buildings**, cite MTF Table 10.4, which splits Tier 4 and 5 by building size
   (up to three rooms or more) [MTF].

## 4. Lighting Global and VeraSol

- **Who.** VeraSol launched in January 2020 as the successor of Lighting Global Quality Assurance; it
  is managed by CLASP and the Schatz Energy Research Center, with Lighting Global as a foundational
  partner [VS-2]. In April 2026 VeraSol announced a "decentralized" model with commercial
  certification partners; Intertek (Hong Kong) is one, providing sampling, testing of kits up to
  350 W to IEC 62257-9-5 and compliance checks to IEC 62257-9-8 [VS-3].
- **What is certified.** Pico-PV products and solar home system kits up to 350 W, tested to
  IEC TS 62257-9-5:2024 and certified to IEC TS 62257-9-8:2025 since the 2025-07-01 transition
  [VS-1]. Certification lasts three years; sample size two units; minimum stock 150 units;
  truth-in-advertising tolerance 10 % for all metrics except light output (15 %) [VS-1].
- **Scope limits of -9-8:2025.** PV maximum power point voltage and all component working voltages
  ≤ 35 V (exceptions for safe AC-to-DC chargers and for larger modules that meet extra safety
  requirements); peak power ≤ 350 W; "only DC outputs and loads. Products that include inverters,
  AC outputs or outlets, or AC appliances are not within the scope"; plug-and-socket connections
  with no tools, no design expertise needed [P-9-8, W-9-8]. It is not a full safety standard and
  does not replace IEC 62368-1, IEC 60335, IEC 62281, UN 38.3 or IEC 61730 [W-9-8]. It does not
  address EMC [P-9-8]. Many such products are type T2I [P-9-8].
- **Appliances.** VeraSol tests (it does not certify, per its own 2025 process) standalone fans,
  refrigerators, solar water pumps (up to 2.4 kW of solar for household or smallholder use),
  electric pressure cookers and productive-use equipment, using Global LEAP test methods (TV, fan,
  refrigerator, solar water pump, electric pressure cooker), and publishes results in the VeraSol
  Product Database [VS-4]. See the conflict note in 1.4.
- **Larger solar home systems.** No IEC or VeraSol quality standard for systems above 350 W, above
  35 V or with inverters was found. -9-6 (comparative selection tests up to 500 Wp) and -200
  (specification tables per component) are the nearest IEC tools [P-9-6, P-200]. For the code:
  Size A kits can require VeraSol certification; larger sizes need their own component rules.

## 5. Suggested code skeleton (my synthesis)

Mirrors the -100 groups [P-100] and the -200 design steps [P-200]:

1. **Plan**: needs survey and load questionnaire (-2, -200 Annex C); resource data (PVGIS, Global
   Solar Atlas, NASA POWER per -200); size choice with its MTF target.
2. **Design**: architecture type (T2I); voltage domain (ELV DC ≤ 120 V Uoc); protection (-5);
   specification per component (-200 clause 8); monitoring plan (-200 clause 9).
3. **Components**: PV modules (IEC 61215, IEC 61730 per -9-6; visual check per PAS -10), PV array
   (IEC 62548), batteries (-341 for flooded lead-acid; lithium rules not in this series), charge
   controllers, inverters (-350), DC balance of system.
4. **Install**: plant and battery enclosure (-9-1), user installation (-9-4 up to 500 VA; -5 above),
   marking and signs (-9-1 clause 10, -341).
5. **Loads and appliances**: lamps (-12-1), VeraSol-tested appliances.
6. **Packaged kits**: VeraSol / -9-8 certified, ≤ 350 W, ≤ 35 V, DC only.
7. **Handover and life**: acceptance in phases with commissioning records (-9-1 clause 8, -6),
   installation manual and user manual (-3 5.8, -9-6 5.2.7), skill levels (-6), maintenance plan
   and records (-6), replacement and recycling (-6, -3 clause 8).
