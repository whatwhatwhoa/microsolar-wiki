## Foreword

This DC solar guide focuses on practical, low-cost designs for solar (PV) systems using commodity
parts. No reinventing the wheel, no Silicon Valley tech startup. Just clear steps to build systems
that households and communities can support in the long term.

DC solar is about removing the waste and complexity that make small systems unstable or
expensive. Done well, DC becomes a reliable backbone for lighting, charging, communication, work,
and essential appliances, especially where grid power is unreliable or unaffordable.

## Mission

**Democratize energy and make it accessible.**

To do this, we follow a few straightforward principles:

- **Use commodity parts.** Keep systems affordable, replaceable, and easy to source anywhere.
- **Make things modular and repairable where it actually helps.** Not everything needs to be
  user-serviceable, but nothing should be disposable.
- **Support community-led deployment.** Systems should be simple enough for local groups to
  install and maintain.
- **Design for long-term sustainability.** Favor efficiency, low idle losses, and durable
  components.
- **Evolve as technology and needs change.** The world shifts; the approach should too.

## Who this guide is for

This guide is for handymen, NGO leaders, and anyone interested in **picogrids**: small, off-grid
systems focused on covering the basics well, storing up to about 5 kWh. It is written for people
who want practical, understandable solutions rather than complex infrastructure, such as homes
and small community buildings in rural and Indigenous communities. It is also a useful reference
for van dwellers and others living with limited, self-contained power systems.

This guide is **not** for large, infrastructure-level projects. It does not target
community-scale or neighborhood-scale grids, utility tie-ins, or high-capex deployments. If your
goal is to build or finance large centralized energy projects, this guide is likely not the
right fit.

## What you should know first

If you plan to install or guide these systems, a basic understanding of electrical fundamentals
is a good foundation:

- Ohm's law
- Current limiting and protection
- How PV systems work

This guide assumes a rudimentary understanding of these topics.
