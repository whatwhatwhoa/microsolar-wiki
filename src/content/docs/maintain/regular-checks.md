These checks need only your eyes, your hand and a cheap multimeter. Write each result in a
notebook kept with the [wiring diagram](/code/labels-and-documents/#sys-21-wiring-diagram).

:::caution
Look and feel, but do not open the distribution board or the battery box unless you are the
installer. Switch the battery off before touching any terminal.
:::

## Every month

1. **Smell and look.** A hot or burning smell, brown marks, melted insulation or a bulging battery
   means: switch the system off and call the installer.
2. **Feel for heat.** With the system working at its usual load, hold the back of your hand near
   (not on) the battery cables, the fuse holders and the board's terminals. A connection much
   warmer than its cable is loose or corroded.
3. **Battery voltage.** Read it in the evening, before the lights go on, and write it down. A value
   that falls month after month means the battery is aging or the panels are not keeping up.
4. **Panels.** Clean dust, bird droppings and leaves off the glass with water and a soft cloth.
   Check that nothing new shades them (growing trees, new buildings).

## Every season

1. **Cables.** Look along every cable you can see for cracks, sun damage, chewing by animals, and
   places where it rubs against an edge.
2. **Fixings.** Panels and mounts are tight; cables are still held at their supports.
3. **Labels.** All labels are still readable ([SYS-2.2](/code/labels-and-documents/#sys-22-labels)).
4. **Spare fuses.** The spares are still there ([SYS-2.4](/code/labels-and-documents/#sys-24-spare-fuses)).
5. **Floating systems.** If the system has no earth connection, the installer checks that no
   conductor has a fault to metal ([DIST-4.4](/code/earthing-and-surge/#dist-44-floating-systems)).

## When a fuse blows

A blown fuse is doing its job. Before replacing it, find out why: a damaged cord, a new device that
draws too much, water in a box. Replace it only with **the same rating**, never a bigger fuse and
never a piece of wire.
