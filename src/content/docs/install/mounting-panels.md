This page is for the installer putting up the solar panels: which way they face, at what angle,
how they are fixed, and how the cable gets down to the charge controller. Your panel maker's
installation manual has the final word on fixings and torque: read it before you start.

:::danger
- **Work at height safely.** Work on a roof only with a safe ladder, fall protection and a second
  person. Do not work in strong wind.
- **Never stand or step on a panel.** It can break the cells inside.
- **A panel makes power as soon as light falls on it.** Keep it face down, or covered with
  opaque board, and keep its connectors apart until the
  [connection order](/install/connection-order/) says to connect them.
:::

## What you need

- The panel maker's installation manual and the mounting rack's instructions.
- A compass (a phone works) and an angle finder, or a phone angle app.
- Bolts, nuts and washers of stainless steel, or the rack maker's own fixings.
- UV-resistant cable clips (stainless steel or UV-rated), a cable gland, and a torque wrench.

## 1. Choose the place

1. **Full sun all year.** From the place, look at where the sun goes in the morning, at noon and in
   the afternoon. Nothing should shade the panels during the sunny hours of the day, in any
   month. Think of trees that will grow and buildings that may be built.
2. **Above the weeds.** On the ground, keep the lowest edge high enough that grass and bushes will
   not shade it.
3. **Short cable run.** Close to the charge controller, so the PV cable is short.

### Why shade matters so much

A panel is made of strings of cells in series: when one cell is in shade, the cells in its group
stop working with it. Shading half of one cell can switch off a third of the panel. A small shadow
can cost more than 30 times its own size in power. The diodes inside the panel limit the loss; they
do not prevent it. One tree branch or a pole is enough.

## 2. Face the equator, at the right angle

1. **Direction.** Face the panels toward the equator: **south** in the northern half of the world,
   **north** in the southern half. Within about 10° is fine. Turning a panel 30° away costs about
   10-15 % of its energy; 60° away costs 20-30 %.
2. **Angle.** Tilt the panel up from flat by about your **latitude** (your distance from the
   equator in degrees). Outside the tropics, tilt 5-15° more than your latitude, to catch the
   low winter sun.
3. **Never flatter than 10°**, even on the equator. Rain then washes the dust off.
4. **Cloudy season?** If one season is very cloudy, tilt for the sunny season instead. The
   off-grid system has to get through its worst month
   ([sun hours](/plan/system-estimator/#sun-hours)).

| Place (example) | Latitude | Tilt |
|---|---|---|
| Near the equator | 0-10° | 10° (the minimum) |
| Suva, Fiji | 18° S | about 18°, facing north |
| Outside the tropics | 30° | about 35-45° |

## 3. Leave air around the panels

1. Keep **at least 10 cm** between the panel frame and the roof or wall behind it. Hot panels
   give less power, and the gap lets leaves and water pass.
2. Leave **at least 10 mm** between panels.
3. Do not block the small drain holes in the panel frame.

## 4. Fix the panels against the wind

:::caution
In a storm a loose panel becomes a flying sheet of glass and aluminium. After a cyclone in
Australia, panels were found up to 250 m from their mounts. Fix every panel for the strongest
wind your area gets.
:::

1. **Fix into the roof structure**, the rafters or purlins, not just the roof sheet. Fixings in the
   sheet alone tear it out in a storm and let water in.
2. **Use the panel's own mounting holes or clamps** in the places its manual allows. **Do not drill
   the frame**: it weakens it and voids the warranty.
3. **At least four clamps per panel.** Clamps hold the frame only, never the glass, and must not
   shade the cells.
4. **Tighten to the maker's torque** with a torque wrench. A typical value is 16-20 N·m for an M8
   bolt; your manual decides.
5. **Same metal, or stainless steel**, for all brackets, bolts and screws. Where two different
   metals touch, separate them with rubber washers, or they corrode.
6. **Wood** must be rated for outdoor use for the life of the system.
7. **Ground mounts** need a rack rated for the local wind too. Fix it to the ground the way the
   rack maker says.
8. **Roof holes** are sealed for the life of the system, with a proper roof flashing where a cable
   or bolt goes through.

## 5. Run the cable

1. **Solar cable and connectors only.** Use solar cable and MC4-type connectors, and connect only
   halves from the **same maker** ([GEN-1.4](/code/solar-array/#gen-14-pv-cable-and-connectors)).
2. **Off the roof, out of water.** Clip the cable and connectors to the rack, not lying on the roof
   where water and leaves collect.
3. **Fixed every 60 cm or less**, so the wind cannot move it. Use stainless steel clips or
   UV-rated clips: ordinary plastic cable ties break in the sun long before the panels wear out,
   and can rub through the cable. The 60 cm comes from the US electrical code (NEC).
4. **Gentle bends.** No bend tighter than 43 mm radius (LONGi; check your panel's manual). Do not
   pull on the cable where it leaves the panel.
5. **Protect it** where people, animals or tools can reach it: run it in UV-rated conduit.

## 6. Bring the cable inside

1. **Drip loop.** Just before the cable enters the wall or a box, let it hang lower than the entry
   point. Rain runs down to the lowest point and drips off instead of following the cable in.
2. **Enter from the side or below**, never through the top of a box.
3. **One cable per hole**, through a cable gland that seals it and grips it. The gland is the
   strain relief: a pull on the cable must not reach the terminals.
4. **Do not rely on silicone** alone to keep water out.
5. Bring the cable to the **PV disconnect** next to the charge controller
   ([GEN-1.3](/code/solar-array/#gen-13-pv-disconnect)).

## 7. Bond the frames

Connect the panel frames and the metal rack to the system's earth point, using the earth holes
marked on the frame ([GEN-1.6](/code/solar-array/#gen-16-frames-and-surge-protection)). Do not drill
extra holes for it.

## When to get help

- Ask a builder or engineer whether an old or light roof can carry the panels and hold the fixings
  in a storm.
- In cyclone or hurricane areas, use a rack rated for your local wind, and follow its instructions
  exactly.
- If you are not trained to work at height, have someone who is fix the panels to the roof.
