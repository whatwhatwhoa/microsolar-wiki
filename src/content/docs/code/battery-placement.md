:::danger
"LFP is safe" is not a reason to put a battery anywhere. A failing LFP battery releases gas that is
flammable (about a third hydrogen) and toxic, often without visible flames first. Where the battery
sits decides whether people can get out.
:::

Australia and New Zealand (AS/NZS 5139) and the US (IRC R328) both apply their placement rules from
**1 kWh** upward, which covers Sizes B and C. The rules below are what they have in common.

## STO-3.1 Never where people sleep or escape

**must** - The battery is not in a bedroom, nor in a cupboard or space that opens into a bedroom,
nor in a corridor, stairway or any route people use to leave the building.

*Why:* people are most at risk where they sleep. A battery fire on the way out blocks the escape.
Both AS/NZS 5139 and IRC R328 exclude these places.

## STO-3.2 Not hidden in the building

**must** - The battery is not in a ceiling space, a wall cavity, under stairs, or under a floor.

*Why:* a fire there spreads unseen through the building's structure (AS/NZS 5139).

## STO-3.3 Good places

**should** - Put the battery in one of these, in order of preference: outdoors or in a separate
shed, in a protected box on an outside wall; in a utility room, store room or garage with
non-combustible walls, separated from living rooms.

*Why:* these are the locations IRC R328 allows. AS/NZS 5139 also keeps batteries away from doors,
windows and vents into living rooms (at least 600 mm to the side, 900 mm below).

## STO-3.4 A barrier to living space

**must** - Between the battery and any living room there is a non-combustible wall or barrier (for
example fibre-cement board at least 6 mm thick, or masonry), and the battery stands on a
non-combustible surface.

*Why:* AS/NZS 5139 (Amendment 1, 2025) asks for a barrier of at least 6 mm of exempt material
between a battery and habitable rooms.

## STO-3.5 Protected, dry and cool

**must** - The battery is fixed in place, out of direct sun, above flood level, away from heaters
and cooking, protected from vehicles, and kept within the temperature range its maker allows.
Children and animals cannot reach its terminals.

*Why:* heat ages a battery and raises fire risk; water and knocks damage it. Moveable shelving is
not protection where a vehicle can reach (AS/NZS 5139).

## STO-3.6 Warn and detect

**must** - A smoke alarm is in the room with the battery. A sign on or next to the battery states
its chemistry, voltage, capacity, and "Do not use water on a battery fire - leave and call for
help". The maker's safety data sheet is kept on site.

*Why:* IRC R328 requires a smoke alarm in the battery room; AS/NZS 5139 Amendment 1 requires the
safety data sheet on site and warning signs for explosive gas and toxic fumes.
