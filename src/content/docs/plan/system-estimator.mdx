List the devices a home or building will run: lights, phones, a laptop, a router, a fan, a fridge.
The estimator adds up the energy they use each day and suggests a battery, solar panels, a charge
controller and, if needed, an inverter. It is for planning and for comparing choices, before you
buy anything.

:::caution
This is an estimate, not a design. It does not choose wire sizes or fuses. Before you buy or wire
anything, check each part against its manual and against [the code](/code/safety-model/).
:::

## Use the estimator

1. Pick an example that looks like your home, or start with an empty list.
2. Add your devices. Change **how many**, **watts** and **hours a day** to match your own.
3. Tick **AC?** only for devices that must plug into a normal wall socket (they need an
   inverter).
4. Set the **sun hours** for your place, and how many days without sun the battery must cover.
5. Read the estimate. **Copy a link** to keep it or send it to an installer, or print it.

The estimator remembers your list in this browser.

## How the estimate works

The estimator does the same steps as [Energy and load budgets](/plan/energy-and-load-budgets/)
and [Sizing panels and batteries](/plan/sizing-panels-and-batteries/), with a little more
detail. You can change every number below under **More settings**.

1. **Energy each device uses**: watts × hours a day × how many. A 5 W bulb for 5 hours is
   25 Wh. Four of them are 100 Wh.
2. **Add the losses** between the battery and the devices. DC devices: divide by 0.9 (step-down
   converters, USB chargers, wire). AC devices: divide by 0.85 (the inverter).
3. **Add the inverter's idle power** if any device needs AC: idle watts × the hours the inverter
   is on. A good 24 V inverter of 500-800 W uses about 8-10 W doing nothing; an AC fridge keeps it
   on for 24 hours, which is 240 Wh a day.
4. **Battery** = energy per day × days without sun ÷ 0.8. You plan to use 80 % of a lithium iron
   phosphate (LiFePO₄) battery, so it keeps a reserve as it ages.
5. **Panels** = energy per day ÷ (sun hours × 0.75 × 0.92). The 0.75 covers heat, dust, wiring
   and the charge controller: a panel rarely gives its label watts on a roof. The 0.92 is the
   battery's round trip: you get back about 92 % of what goes in.
6. **Charge controller** = panel watts ÷ battery volts × 0.975. This is the most current the
   panels can push into the battery. Pick the next size up.
7. **Everything on at once**: the sum of all watts, and the current that takes from the battery.
   Each DC device also gets its [branch class](/code/overcurrent-protection/#dist-15-branch-classes)
   (limited, standard or power). Fridges take about twice their running power for a moment when
   the compressor starts.
8. **Inverter**, if needed: at least 25 % above the AC devices' watts, with a peak rating that
   covers a motor starting.
9. **Size and tier**: the [system size](/plan/system-sizes/) follows from the battery
   (up to 0.5 kWh is A, 2.5 kWh is B, 5 kWh is C). The MTF tier shows which
   [Multi-Tier Framework](/plan/system-sizes/#sizes-and-the-multi-tier-framework) capacity level
   your list of loads needs.

The system voltage, when you let the estimator choose, is 12 V for a small kit (up to 0.5 kWh
and 150 W), 48 V above 5 kWh or 1 500 W, and 24 V for everything between. See
[Choose a system voltage](/plan/choose-a-system-voltage/).

### Sun hours

**Sun hours** (peak sun hours) are the hours of full, strong sun a panel gets in a day, added up.
Use the **worst month** in which you need the power, not the yearly average. Find them for your
place on [Global Solar Atlas](https://globalsolaratlas.info/), [PVGIS](https://re.jrc.ec.europa.eu/pvg_tools/en/)
or [NASA POWER](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/). In most tropical places the worst month gives
3 to 5 sun hours; the estimator starts at 4.

### Days without sun

One day of battery suits most homes: the panels refill it the next day. Choose 2 where cloudy
spells are common. Loads that must never stop need far more: the World Health Organization asks
at least 3 days, and 5 without good local weather data, for a solar vaccine fridge, plus 25 % more
panel.

## Worked example

The example "Home with laptop and internet", with 4 sun hours:

| Device | How many | Watts | Hours | Wh a day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LED bulb | 4 | 5 | 5 | 100 |
| Night light | 1 | 1 | 10 | 10 |
| Smartphone charging | 4 | 10 | 1.5 | 60 |
| Laptop | 1 | 20 | 4 | 80 |
| Wi-Fi router | 1 | 10 | 24 | 240 |
| Table fan | 1 | 10 | 9 | 90 |
| Starlink Mini | 1 | 30 | 6 | 180 |
| **Total** | | | | **760** |

- From the battery: 760 Wh ÷ 0.9 = **844 Wh a day**.
- Battery: 844 Wh × 1 day ÷ 0.8 = **1.06 kWh**. At 24 V that is 1 056 ÷ 25.6 = 41 Ah: one
  24 V 50 Ah pack.
- Panels: 844 Wh ÷ (4 h × 0.75 × 0.92) = **306 W**: one 450 W panel, with room to spare.
- Charge controller: 450 W ÷ 25.6 V × 0.975 = 17 A: a **20 A MPPT** controller.
- Everything on at once: 131 W, about 6 A from the battery.
- Result: **Size B**. The router, on all day, uses more energy than anything else.

## Typical devices

These are the starting values in the estimator. The watts come from lab tests and makers' data
(see the source column); the hours are planning guesses. Your own device's label or manual is
better: look for watts (W), or volts × amps (V × A).

:::note
Makers often test fridges in a cool room (21-25 °C). In a 32 °C room the same fridge can use three
times as much. Use a lab value tested at 32 °C where you can find one.
:::

## Ways to need less

- **Keep it DC.** An inverter wastes energy just by being on ([LOAD-1.3](/code/outputs-and-loads/#load-13-dc-loads-first)).
- **Choose a DC fridge** over an AC fridge on an inverter ([the fridge problem](/plan/energy-and-load-budgets/#the-fridge-problem)).
- **Put always-on devices on a timer.** A router or satellite dish that runs 24 hours is often the
  biggest load in a small home.
- **Use heavy loads in the middle of the day**, when the sun is strong, not at night.
- **Leave cooking and heating out.** Kettles, irons, hair dryers and electric stoves need far more
  power than these systems give.

## What the estimate does not do

- Choose wires and fuses: see [DIST-1](/code/overcurrent-protection/),
  [DIST-2](/code/wiring/) and the main fuse rule [STO-2.4](/code/battery-fuse-and-isolator/#sto-24-size-the-main-fuse-to-its-cable).
- Check the panel voltage in cold weather: see [GEN-1.1](/code/solar-array/#gen-11-panel-voltage-in-the-cold).
- Allow for shade. One tree branch can cut a panel's output by a third or more: mount panels in
  full sun.
- Plan for growth. If you expect to add a fridge or more rooms later, choose a battery and
  controller with room to grow.

## When to get help

Get an experienced installer or a licensed electrician if the estimate shows **Size D**, more than
1 500 W at once, an inverter above 1 000 W, or anything that connects to a building's mains
wiring. Wiring rules differ by country.
